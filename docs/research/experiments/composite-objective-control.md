# Composite objective and denominator controls

The frozen [CPU control](../../../benchmarks/composite_objective_control.py) confirms two known method boundaries. [Protocol](composite-objective-protocol.md), [raw results](composite-objective-control.json), [positive/negative tests](../../../tests/test_composite_objective_control.py). These authored finite float64 cases supply neither independent human labels nor a new checker contribution.

For a two-head objective on seven examples partitioned 4+3, independently summing each head's numerator and dividing by its own global valid count matches the full objective and gradients. Maximum gradient error is 1.39e-17 in all three cases. Naively weighting local head means by example fraction gives maximum gradient errors .1163, .1163 and .1197. Equal global head counts do not repair unequal per-microbatch valid-count proportions.

When the first microbatch has no valid start labels but the complete logical batch does, a local mean is nonfinite while its gradients remain finite in this fixture. The summed/global-denominator repair stays finite. The diagnostic records this coexistence but refuses the optimizer step after nonfinite loss; finite gradients cannot replace required loss-health checks. An entirely undefined global head mean receives an explicit unsupported outcome.

For a parameter-dependent normalizer, full and detached-denominator forward losses are both .6735016331613176, but their gradient maximum difference is .4950. Streaming numerator and denominator derivatives combined with the quotient rule agree with full autograd within 1.11e-16; independent central finite differences agree within 2.99e-11. This demonstrates the known missing derivative term, not a generic solution for arbitrary coupled or prediction-dependent losses.

The actual QA resource screen has no ignored endpoints, so its feature-count weighting is supported by its inspected loss semantics. The control prevents overgeneralizing that special case. It does not establish that the resource pilot has a denominator bug, that TrainParity cannot consume a correctly authored composite loss, or that Atlas alone can enforce these rules. The [interface amendment](../../architecture/objective-and-evidence-boundaries.md) supplies global logical-batch context, explicit per-term units and an unsupported boundary for unreviewed model-dependent normalizers.
